Follow-up has a trigger.

The book replaces generic “just checking in” behavior with a Message Trigger Sequence that ties communication to a specific condition.

1. DetectSpot the status change.
2. MatchIdentify the trigger category.
3. DecideWait, ask, confirm, or escalate.
4. SendMessage only when the condition calls for it.

Red, Amber, Green—without guesswork.

Risk becomes useful only when the color changes what you do next.

Green

Current, owned, moving.
Maintain the record and leave it alone unless the due date changes.

Amber

Approaching risk.
Prevent: send the targeted reminder, confirm the missing piece, or reset timing.

Red

Needs intervention now.
Act: escalate, request a decision, or remove the blockage.

Preserve the evidence

The record behind the reminder.

A clear Approval Record gives you a defensible decision history. Instead of saying “I thought someone confirmed that,” you can show the request, decision, date and responsible stakeholder.

Approval Record

✓Request — what was asked or sent.
✓Decision — approved, declined, revised, or still waiting.
✓Date — when the request or decision happened.
✓Stakeholder — who owns the decision.
